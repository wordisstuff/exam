# Minnesota QB question-writing standard

## Non-negotiable editorial rules

1. **Original content only.** Write independent practice questions. Never use leaked questions, brain dumps, reconstructed confidential questions, copied or near-copied DLI samples, or claim that an item appeared on an exam. The DLI guide's samples inform format only.
2. **English first.** The English prompt uses professional code/licensing language. Translate the **question only** into Ukrainian; answer choices remain English.
3. **Five answers.** Every new serious (`draft` or `reviewed`) bank item has exactly five plausible choices. Four-choice records are permitted only as legacy/sample learning content. Never use absurd filler.
4. **Stable IDs, not letters.** Correctness uses stable answer IDs. Letters are presentation only.
5. **Single best answer.** A single-choice item has exactly one clearly best answer under its cited source.
6. **Multiple select.** State the number explicitly (for example, “Which THREE…”), store that number, and award no partial credit.
7. **Negative wording.** `NOT`, `EXCEPT`, `does not`, and `is not permitted` may be realistic but must not be overused. Emphasize the operative negative.
8. **Application.** Include field and plan-review scenarios, not vocabulary recall alone. Some exam-level items may include irrelevant facts consistent with the published format.
9. **Calculations.** Include appropriate inch-pound dimensions, areas, percentages, loads, and construction math, with units and a teachable solution.
10. **Explanation.** Teach why the answer follows and, where useful, why distractors fail.
11. **References and verification.** Every reviewed item has a source-checked reference with source, section, and optional subsection/title/note. Structural validity is not technical/legal verification.
12. **No unsourced precision.** Never invent a dimension, exception, amount, deadline, code number, or legal duty from memory. Keep an item `draft`/`unverified` until a reviewer checks the controlling primary source, Minnesota amendments, exceptions, and effective edition.

## Difficulty

- `easy`: direct recognition or a basic rule.
- `medium`: understanding or application is required.
- `exam`: scenario, exception, cross-reading, negative wording, calculation, multiple concepts, or realistic distractors.

Difficulty is independent of language tags. Use tags such as `shall`, `may`, `except`, `NOT`, `minimum`, `maximum`, `not less than`, `not more than`, `at least`, `no greater than`, `permitted`, `required`, `prohibited`, `unless`, `except as otherwise permitted`, `greatest concern`, `least`, `most appropriate`, and `best` only when the wording appears or materially affects interpretation. Do not tag indiscriminately.

## Promotion checklist

Promote `draft` to `reviewed` and `unverified` to `source-checked` only after an editor confirms originality, English/Ukrainian prompt fidelity, five English options, answer IDs, selection count, explanation, taxonomy, exact reference locator, controlling Minnesota text and amendments, exceptions, and the correct answer. A reviewed/source-checked record still becomes Full Exam eligible only if runtime structural checks pass.


## Minnesota residential-code source hierarchy

For every residential-code item, verify the **2018 IRC base provision incorporated by Minnesota**, then check **Minnesota Rules Chapter 1309** for a replacement, deletion, modification, addition, or exception, and check **Chapter 1300 or another cross-referenced Minnesota State Building Code chapter** where applicable. “2020 Minnesota Residential Code” is a readable combined label, not permission to rely on an unamended generic IRC. Do not substitute the 2021 or 2024 IRC. Record the exact controlling locator and amendment relationship in the reference or audit record before promotion.
